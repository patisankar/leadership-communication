Speaking feedback
=============
Use shorter sentences. One idea at a time.

-Slow down when you speak and pause between ideas.

-Be careful with grammar (verbs, prepositions, and articles).

-Repetition of words like so, if, and makes it harder to follow.

-Try to organize your talk like this:

1. The problem

2. One example

3. The new way of thinking

4. The result

2nd Feedback
==========
Your main idea is strong, but your sentences are too long. Try to express one idea per sentence to improve clarity. You mention three questions, which is great, but make them clearer by saying:

“The first question is…”, “The second…”, “The third…”

Also, watch small grammar points (verb tense, articles, prepositions). They don’t block meaning, but fixing them will make you sound more polished. 
Your example is good, but for speaking, shorter examples work better. Make sure you slow down slightly and pause between ideas to sound more confident.

3rd Feedback
=========

1.** Shorter sentences sound clearer and more senior.**
   Before (long, less senior):
“We made this change because the SDK couldn’t always load the key and in those cases we logged a warning and continued, which caused confusion for merchants.”

After (short, senior):
“The SDK could not always load the key. Logging a warning and continuing caused confusion for merchants.”

Practice rule:
If a sentence has more than one “and,” split it.

This habit alone will noticeably improve clarity and perceived seniority in meetings, PR reviews, and design discussions.

3. **Less self-correction while speaking**
Saying things like “sorry,” “I lost it,” “I’m thinking” breaks your flow. It’s better to pause silently instead.

4. Clear verbs
Use simple, strong verbs:

“My responsibility is to deliver technically and to communicate clearly.”

“I will focus on data, not personal opinions.”

4. Pronunciation & clarity
Speak a bit slower, especially for:

*cross-functional

*collaboration

*discussion
