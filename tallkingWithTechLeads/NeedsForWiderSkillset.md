### Crossfunctional Conflicts
**Situation**
In cross-functional projects, I often worked with engineers and stakeholders from diverse backgrounds, where communication styles and expectations varied significantly.

**Task**
As a senior engineer, my responsibility was not only to deliver technically, but also to ensure conversations remained clear, professional, and constructive—especially during design reviews, 
feedback discussions, or disagreements.

**Action**
Based on feedback from tech leads and insights from a leadership book, I became more deliberate in how I communicated. 
I focused on reframing conversations away from opinions or personal interpretations and toward data, measurable impact, and desired outcomes.
Specifically, I clarified intent early, separated professional feedback from personal context, and anchored discussions in facts such as metrics, trade-offs, risks, and business impact. 
When addressing sensitive topics, I used outcome-oriented language to keep conversations objective and non-confrontational.

**Result**
As a result, collaboration improved noticeably. Discussions became more productive, conflicts de-escalated faster, and feedback was better received. 
This helped build trust within the team and enabled smoother delivery across multiple initiatives.
