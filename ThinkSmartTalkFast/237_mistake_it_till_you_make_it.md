Astro Teller is redefining **storytelling**.

He’s not talking about persuasion, hype, or marketing. He’s talking about using communication to create a **shared architecture of understanding**—especially when working on complex, uncertain problems.

In practical terms, that means effective communication answers three questions:

1. **Where are we trying to go?**  
   What is the goal or outcome, stated clearly and concretely?

2. **Why do we believe it’s possible?**  
   What assumptions, evidence, or hypotheses suggest we can get from here to there?

3. **How should we interpret signals along the way?**  
   What will success, failure, and learning look like as we experiment and iterate?

For senior engineers, this is critical. Most confusion on teams doesn’t come from lack of intelligence or effort—it comes from misaligned mental models. People are working hard, but toward different interpretations of the same problem.

Teller’s point is that **good communication builds alignment before execution**. It helps teams reason together under uncertainty, make better decisions, and adapt without chaos.

**In short:**  
Storytelling isn’t about excitement.  
It’s about clarity, shared understanding, and disciplined thinking in complex systems.
